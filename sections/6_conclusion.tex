\section{Conclusion}
We presented \name{}, an alignment-enhancing technique inspired by MoE's per-input routing principle. The same mechanism through which MoE achieves capability efficiency, the input-conditioned selection of a sparse expert subset, can be repurposed to enhance alignment at no training cost, by deriving per-query identification of risk-amplifying experts and a risk-modulated suppression bias from a single shared probe. Across three MoE backbones, \name{} improves average safety by $21.2$, $21.7$, and $23.0$ percentage points respectively, while preserving XSTest helpfulness within $0$\,pp of the unmodified backbone. The suppress-amplify asymmetry we report further suggests that alignment in MoE is not a question of choosing between safe and unsafe experts on equal footing, but of selectively muting the experts that, on this specific input, push the model toward unsafe behavior.
