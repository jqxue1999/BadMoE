\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why suppression works but amplification fails.} The
concordance analysis (Figure~\ref{fig:ablations-overview}~(b)) and the amplification
pilot (\S\ref{sec:negresult}) line up to tell a consistent story.
Per-query $\Delta_{\text{beh}}$ negative identifies experts whose role
is to suppress the refusal direction at a probe point, which aligns
with SteerMoE's population-level A-minus because both methods are
picking up a robust disruption signal. Per-query positive
$\Delta_{\text{beh}}$, in contrast, identifies experts whose marginal
activation would push the residual toward the refusal direction, but
this is a counterfactual direction rather than a feature the model is
actually using to refuse. Forcing these experts to fire at amplitude
($+3$ router bias) pushes the residual stream into regions the model
does not know how to decode, producing gibberish. Suppression has no
such failure mode because it merely reduces already-active disruptive
experts' contribution.

\paragraph{PAIR underperformance.} PAIR is the one attack where
$C_{\text{perQ-sup}}$ trails baseline by $-3$pp. PAIR queries are
optimized by an adversarial LLM to produce prompts that look benign on
the surface; $\Delta_{\text{beh}}$ on these prompts has intermediate
magnitude and the selected experts resemble those selected on
\emph{none} more than on GCG or past-tense. One hypothesis is that
PAIR queries evade the ``refusal engagement'' that our method depends
on. A larger $n$ is needed to confirm whether this is a true
regression or noise within the $\pm 14$pp 95\% CI at $n{=}50$.

\paragraph{Bandwidth comparison.} $C_{\text{perQ-sup}}$ uses $K{=}25$
negative experts only — half the total intervention bandwidth of
SteerMoE ($K{=}25$ activate $+$ $K{=}25$ deactivate). That the
suppression-only method still outperforms suggests SteerMoE's
amplification side is either redundant or, as our
$D_{\text{perQ-comb}}$ pilot hints, silently trading helpfulness
for negligible safety gain.

\paragraph{Limitations.} All experiments are on OLMoE-1B-7B, so whether
the same per-query signal structure exists on DeepSeek-V2-Lite,
Qwen-MoE, or Mixtral is an immediate follow-up. Bypass attacks such as
deepinception and cipher do not engage refusal circuitry at all at the
probe layer ($\|\Delta_{\text{beh}}\| \to 0$), leaving our method with
no signal to act on; Appendix~\ref{app:bypass} confirms this is
independent of suppression strength. Although we cache $\Delta(q)$
offline in our experiments, a deployed system would incur one extra
forward-backward pass per query (about $5\%$ of greedy generation cost
for $256$ tokens), with the method remaining compatible with batching.
Finally, our $n{=}50$ per cell yields 95\% binomial CIs of about
$\pm14$pp on safety rates, so differences under this gap are
underpowered; extending to $n{=}100$ is a natural next step.

\section{Conclusion}

Different inputs recruit different adversarial experts in
Mixture-of-Experts language models. We quantified this input-specificity
(Jaccard $0.11$ between per-query top-5 sets on $200$ queries), and
designed a two-step alignment enhancement that respects it: locate per-query
adversarial experts with a single causal autograd through router
biases, then suppress them with a constant router-bias shift at
generation. The method is weight-preserving, adds only a single
forward-backward pass per query, and at identical intervention
bandwidth outperforms the strong SteerMoE baseline on jailbreak safety
while incurring zero over-refusal cost on XSTest benign prompts. The
suppress-amplify asymmetry we report closes the loop. The ``unsafe''
direction of our per-query signal has concordant evidence from a
population-level pool (SteerMoE's $\mathcal{A}^{-}$), the ``safe''
direction does not, and amplification accordingly fails. MoE safety
steering is, at bottom, a question of which adversarial experts to
silence on this input.
