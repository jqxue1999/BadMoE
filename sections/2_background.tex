\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{MoE Transformer Architectures.}
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models~\cite{shazeer2017outrageously, fedus2022switch, zoph2022stmoe} replace the dense feed-forward sublayer of a transformer with $E$ parallel expert sub-networks at each MoE layer. For each token, a learned \textit{router} selects a sparse top-$k$ subset of experts whose outputs are combined to produce the layer's output, conditioning the active computation on the input. Modern MoE architectures vary in scale and granularity but instantiate the same routing mechanism in which the \textit{router} alone determines which experts contribute. Empirically, this per-input selection induces expert specialization along axes such as syntactic role, domain, and reasoning style~\cite{riquelme2021scaling}.

\noindent\textbf{Inference-time alignment-enhancing techniques.}
A growing body of work enhances the alignment of pretrained LLMs at inference time without modifying weights. At the activation level, \citet{arditi2024refusal} identified a one-dimensional refusal direction in residual streams whose orthogonalization ablates safety behavior, with subsequent work extending this to persona traits~\cite{chen2025persona} and broader behavioral steering~\cite{subramani2022extracting, turner2023activation, rimsky2024caa, marks2023geometry}. At the decoding level, RAIN~\cite{li2024rain} performs rewindable autoregressive inference with self-evaluation, SafeDecoding~\cite{xu2024safedecoding} biases token-level logits toward safer continuations, and contrastive decoding methods~\cite{li2023contrastive, obrien2023contrastive} rerank generation against a reference model. These approaches share a common abstraction: they treat the model as a dense computational pathway and apply the same intervention machinery to every input. \name{} introduces routing-stage intervention that exploits MoE's per-input expert selection as a new mechanism in this family.
