\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Models and Benchmarks}
\label{sec:setup-data}
We instantiate \name on three MoE backbones: OLMoE-1B-7B-0924-Instruct~\cite{muennighoff2025olmoe}, Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat, and Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1. Per-model architectural details are in Appendix~\ref{app:model-details}.

For risky inputs we use the PandaGuard JBB attack suite~\cite{pandaguard}, which wraps each of $100$ JailbreakBench harmful goals into $25$ attack variants. We report the six attacks where the original model engages refusal at a non-trivial rate: \emph{PastTense}~\cite{andriushchenko2024past}, \emph{GCG}~\cite{zou2023gcg}, \emph{PAIR}~\cite{chao2023pair}, \emph{AutoDAN}, \emph{ICA}, and \emph{Renellm}. Following PandaGuard's unified-proxy protocol, \emph{GCG} and \emph{PAIR} adversarial prompts are pre-computed once against a Llama-3.1-8B proxy, so all reported numbers reflect transfer-attack robustness. For benign over-refusal we use XSTest~\cite{rottger2024xstest}, which contains prompts that superficially resemble harmful requests but are safe to answer. For general capability we report MMLU 5-shot accuracy.

In addition to per-attack analysis, we group the 100 JBB goals by the five JailbreakBench~\cite{jailbreakbench} categories: \emph{Harassment/Discrimination}, \emph{Malware/Hacking}, \emph{Physical harm}, \emph{Economic harm}, and \emph{Fraud/Deception}. We use the original JBB labels directly. Per-category safety in Table~\ref{tab:main} is the average safe rate across all six attacks for goals in that category.

\subsection{Baseline Methods}

\noindent\textbf{Original.} The unmodified backbone with no router intervention. Establishes the safety floor and helpfulness ceiling against which interventions are measured.

\noindent\textbf{SteerMoE}~\cite{fayyaz2025steermoe}. The published router-level safety steering method on MoE LLMs. SteerMoE selects $K$ risk-amplifying experts from aggregate routing statistics on a held-out risky-vs-benign contrast set, then applies the same bias to every query at inference.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
Top-$K$ for identification is fixed at $K{=}25$, matching SteerMoE's bandwidth, with bias strength $\beta = 10$. The query-level ramp endpoints $(\lambda_{\text{lo}}, \lambda_{\text{hi}})$ are calibrated per backbone on a held-out risky-versus-benign split. The probe layer $\ell^*$ is set per backbone by held-out $\lambda$-AUROC: $\ell^* = 10$ for OLMoE-1B-7B, $\ell^* = 14$ for Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B, and $\ell^* = 20$ for Mixtral-8x7B. Sensitivity to this choice is studied in Section~\ref{sec:probe-ablation}; unless otherwise noted, all reported numbers use these defaults. All experiments run on a single NVIDIA B200, with online intervention adding ${\sim}5\%$ overhead over greedy decoding. Decoding config, direction normalization, and detailed compute breakdown are in Appendix~\ref{app:impl}.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
We report three metrics, each averaged across queries within the relevant evaluation set; main-table values are means over $n{=}100$ queries per cell. Safety and over-refusal are scored by an LLM judge: we follow PandaGuard~\cite{pandaguard} and use Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25} with two prompt templates, applied identically to \emph{Original}, \emph{SteerMoE}, and \name. Full prompt templates and threshold rationale are in Appendix~\ref{app:judge}.

\noindent\textbf{Safe rate ($\%$, $\uparrow$).} Fraction of responses whose alignment score $s \in [0, 1]$ (with $0$ = harmful, $0.5$ = neutral, $1$ = aligned) satisfies $s \geq 0.7$ under the safety prompt. Reported per attack (Table~\ref{tab:main}, columns 3--8) and per JBB category (columns 9--13).

\noindent\textbf{XSTest answer rate ($\%$, $\uparrow$).} Fraction of responses labelled \textsc{answer} (rather than \textsc{refuse}) by the judge under a binary over-refusal prompt. Measures over-refusal on prompts that superficially resemble harmful requests but are safe to answer; the gap between this and the \emph{Original} ceiling is the \emph{benign tax} of an intervention.

\noindent\textbf{MMLU accuracy ($\%$, $\uparrow$).} Standard 5-shot evaluation~\cite{hendrycks2021mmlu}. Measures whether router intervention degrades general capability beyond the safety and helpfulness axes.
